# Anti-slop: what the community rejects, and how to avoid it

"Slop" in the community means low-effort, mass-produced material that shows no decisions behind it. Merriam-Webster
made "slop" its 2025 Word of the Year, defined as low-quality digital material, usually mass-produced with AI
(TechCrunch, Dec 2025). The objection is to missing human judgment and undisclosed generation, not to any tool.
This file gives the evidence, the policies, the tells, and the gates a mod must pass.

## What the community objects to (evidence)

- **Most gamers are negative about generative AI in games.** Quantic Foundry's survey (Oct–Dec 2025, N = 1,799,
  self-selected from its audience): 85% below-neutral, 63% the most negative option. Story-focused players were more
  negative. Respondents were more open to AI inside gameplay than to AI making art or audio. The sample is not
  representative of all gamers.
- **Hollow AI dialogue is a named complaint.** Players and critics describe AI NPC lines as repetitive, flat and
  emotionally empty (forums and postmortem discussions, reported in search results; not measured).
- **Undisclosed use is the core complaint.** In the RPG Maker community, the main objection in a widely shared
  itch.io post is dishonesty about how assets were made, plus the replacement of human artists. Players called out
  suspected AI in Identity V's promotional material, and in Crimson Desert's assets when its Steam page had no AI
  disclosure (search results).
- **Fan-community norms.** Anime conventions (FanX, Anime Los Angeles) have barred AI art from vendor floors and
  artist alleys, citing originality standards (Axios, Sportskeeda; search results).
- **Accepted when disclosed.** One developer reports that being upfront about AI use kept backlash minimal; another
  says most players of Stormgate were fine with AI-animated portraits while a vocal minority objected. Disclosure
  reduces the damage; it does not remove the objection.

## Policies that apply to shipping a mod

|---|---|---|
| itch.io | Generative-AI use in asset packs must be tagged. Untagged assets lose browse-page indexing, enforced partly by user reports. | PC Gamer and Game Developer reports (search results; the exact wording was not opened) |
| Steam | Requires disclosure of AI use at submission (reported). | search results (check the current Steam form) |
| Nexus Mods | AI-generated mod content is not banned; it may be removed after a credible complaint from a rights holder. Creators are advised to avoid AI tools unless they have explicit permission for every asset, especially voices. Tags for AI-Generated Content, AI Media and AI Assisted apply. | Nexus Mods news (search result) |
| Modrinth | Bans fully AI-generated projects; requires disclosure of partly generative content ("Contains AI-generated content"). | Modrinth "AI policy and disclosures" (search result, dated Aug 2026) |
| mods.one | Not found in search. Check its rules before uploading. | — |
| OMORI community | OMOCAT's own stance on generative AI was not found. Check the OMORI Steam news, the developer's channels and the Discord before assuming either way. | — |
| US Copyright Office | Report, Jan 29 2025 (Part 2): output made entirely by generative AI has no copyright; human selection, arrangement or modification can be protected, but only the human-contributed part. Registration requires disclosure of AI material beyond de minimis. | Copyright Office report summaries (law-firm summaries, search results; read copyright.gov) |

What this means for a mod:
- An AI-generated sprite or track is not yours to protect, and you cannot claim it is original work.
- Disclose any generated material in the credits file and on the upload page. Say what the human did (chose,
  arranged, edited, drew over it).
- Check each host's current rule on the day you upload.

## The skill's default rules (for anyone using this skill)

These are the skill's rules, chosen to keep the person as director. They are not a legal opinion or a claim about
OMORI's developer.

1. **Human-decided text.** Dialogue, choices and names are written or rewritten by the person, from a blueprint
   (`director.md`). Generated text may be used only as a rough draft the person rewrites. Never ship unread output.
2. **Art is never generated by an AI image model.** Use the game's own art first, then CC0 and licensed libraries
   checked for licence and style fit, and the person's own drawing or editing (`asset-sourcing.md`). The agent searches,
   checks, records and tests fit; it does not make the picture.
3. **Human-made or licensed audio.** Vanilla OMORI audio first, then free licences (CC0, CC-BY) with credit.
4. **Disclosure.** Any generated material is named in the credits file, with what the human changed.
5. **No undisclosed reuse.** Every third-party file has author, URL and licence in the credits file.
6. **Proof of decision.** The blueprint for each scene exists and matches what shipped.

## Tells of slop, by medium, with a test for each

### Text
Research sources (Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" list, reported in search results) name words that show up far
more in machine text: delve, intricate, tapestry, pivotal, underscore, landscape, foster, testament, enhance, crucial.
Other tells: triplet lists by default; filler openers ("it is important to note", "in today's fast-paced world");
heavy bolding of key terms; lines that state the theme outright; characters who explain their own feelings.
- **Caveat.** These appear in human writing too, and word lists go stale (one source says "delve" faded through 2025).
  A list is a signal to read the line again, not proof.
- **Em dashes.** A weak signal. NPR's 2025 coverage of the "save the em dash" debate shows it is a contested tell.
  Use density and placement as the test, not presence.
- **Test: the scene's voice.** Read the line aloud. Would this character say it, in this room, at this moment?
  Ingold's test: real people do not say "Did you ever stop to think about the implications of that?" (Radiator
  blog, notes on his AdventureX 2018 talk).
- **Test: subtext.** What does the line mean beyond its words? If nothing, rewrite it.

### Art and sprites
- Palette or line weight that does not match the base game's sheets next to it.
- Wrong frame counts: a `%(n)` that does not divide the sheet width (`mod lint` catches it).
- Feet not on the floor line; characters that float or sink against the same ground.
- Lighting or shadows that point in different directions within one scene.
- Frame timing that is uniform (every frame the same length). Animation timing matters more than frame count
  (see `sprites-and-animation.md`).
- Details that do not survive a second look: extra fingers, unreadable props, text that is gibberish.
- **Test.** Put the new sprite next to three base-game sprites at the same zoom. If it looks foreign, it is.

### Audio
- Music that does not change with the scene (a single loop under every beat).
- Loops with an audible seam; a fade that starts or ends at the wrong point.
- Loudness that jumps between tracks. Silence that happens by accident, not because someone chose it.
- **Test.** Play the scene with the music off. Does it still work? Then the music is adding, not covering.

### Design and structure
- Filler: a scene with no GOAL, no CHANGES, or no CUT IF.
- Restated beats: the same information given twice.
- Effects left on after their moment (vignette, tint, weather, tremble).
- Choices whose branches end in the same place.
- Unexplained blockers and dead ends; a route nobody can finish.
- Mechanics added for padding rather than for the scene's goal.

## Gates before a mod is called finished

- [ ] Every scene has a blueprint with GOAL, CHANGES and CUT IF filled in.
- [ ] Every generated or AI-assisted asset is listed in the credits file, with what the human did.
- [ ] Every third-party file has author, URL and licence.
- [ ] Dialogue read aloud: each line passes the voice and subtext tests above.
- [ ] `mod lint` has no errors, and its style warnings are read and either fixed or justified.
- [ ] A full playthrough from a clean save, with the director's review done (`director.md`).
- [ ] Upload rules for the target host checked on the day of upload.
