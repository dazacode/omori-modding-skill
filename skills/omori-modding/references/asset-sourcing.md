# Asset sourcing for a solo modder

The person is one person with limited time, and the agent's job is to find, check, record and fit assets, not to make
them. The skill's policy is that **art is never generated by an AI image model**. Sound and text follow the same
spirit: they come from the game, from licensed libraries, or from the person. This file is the order to look in, the
licence checks, the style-fit reality, and the record that keeps credits correct.

Confidence labels: **[read]** a primary page opened; **[search]** a search result only; **[unverified]** not found.

## 1. The order to look in (cheapest and safest first)
1. **The game's own assets.** OMORI's sprites, pictures, tiles, audio and fonts already match the style. Reuse, recolour,
   cut or recompose them (`art-assets.md` sections 1 and 4). This is where most solo mods should start. Measured sizes
   are in `art-assets.md`; list what exists with `mod art sizes` and `mod say`/`mod find` (OpenOmori).
2. **CC0 libraries** (public domain, no credit needed; credit still recommended in the record). Kenney is the reference
   [read]: "all game assets on the asset pages are public domain licensed (CC0)", commercial use allowed, attribution
   "not required" but welcome, and the Kenney logo is reserved [read].
3. **CC-BY or OGA-BY** assets. Credit is required. OGA-BY drops the DRM restriction; CC-BY forbids adding DRM [read].
4. **CC-BY-SA or GPL** assets. Share-alike: a derivative must be released under the same licence [read on OGA; the
   Freesound page does not cover it]. Avoid these where a CC0 or CC-BY option does the same job, because they can spread
   their licence to your work.
5. **Make it by hand**, or commission a human artist. The person draws or edits; the agent can write the tool that
   makes a repeated element (`art-assets.md` section 5).
6. **AI image generation: not used.** Not as a shortcut, not as a placeholder, not for a "rough" that ships.

## 2. Style fit: tested, not assumed
OMORI is a **3/4 view with 32 px tiles and 32 px character cells**, measured in this install [measured]. Free
packs differ: Kenney's Tiny Farm is 16×16 [search]; the 1,700-tile roguelike pack is 16×16 [search]; 0x72's
DungeonTileset II, Puny World and Anokolisa's pack are 16×16, top-down [search; 0x72 and Puny World read on their pages].

**The scaling test [tested]** (a render, not a guess). The CC0 Puny dungeon tileset (16 px, read on OpenGameArt) was
scaled 2× with nearest-neighbour and placed next to OMORI's 32 px tiles from `BS_Faraway_Exterior`, decoded with
`mod art decode`, at the same on-screen cell size. Result:
- A 2× integer scale is **clean** (no blur), so the asset does not look broken.
- But every art pixel of the 16 px asset is **twice the size** of an OMORI art pixel. The stone bricks read as coarse blocks
  beside OMORI's flat, fine-grained tiles, so the two look like different games.
- The grey stone palette does not match OMORI's pastel greens and yellows.
- Conclusion: a 16 px asset can serve as a **reference** or for an element outside OMORI's style. It does not fit OMORI's
  world as it stands. Only a 32 px asset, or a hand redraw at 32 px, fits the pixel density.

**What fits (verified)**
- **Eldiran's "32x32 RPG Character Sprites"** on OpenGameArt: **CC0**, 32×32, 20 characters with walk and standing poses
  [read]. It is the first free sprite set found at OMORI's cell size. Its fit to OMORI's 3/4 view and palette is **not yet
  tested**: run the same render test before use.
- **Pipoya's 32×32 packs are not CC0** [read]: commercial and personal use and editing are allowed, but "Not redistribute
  or resell this assets." A mod ships the files to players, so this forbids them in a shipped mod. Use them only if the
  pack's terms are read and they are not shipped (for example as a reference you redraw from).

**Sound fits well.** Freesound and CC0 effects are scale-free (section 4).

**Fonts and palettes** are a separate check. The OMORI font and the game's own palette (`img/system/Windows.png`) come
first. For Lospec palettes (see section 3), the terms do not state a licence for palettes (below).

**Backgrounds and parallaxes** from CC0 packs can work if re-tinted into OMORI's palette; run the same render test.

## 3. The licence check (the agent runs this for every asset)
Read the licence **on the asset's own page**, not on a mirror, a repost or a search snippet. Record the answer.

| Licence | Credit | Commercial sale | Share-alike | DRM | What to put in the record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC0 | not required | yes | no | fine | source URL, author, date, "CC0" |
| CC-BY 3.0 or 4.0 | required | yes | no | not allowed | the full credit line below |
| OGA-BY 3.0 or 4.0 | required | yes | no | allowed | the full credit line |
| CC-BY-SA 3.0 or 4.0 | required | yes | yes: derivatives under the same licence | not allowed | the credit line, and the list of derivatives |
| GPL 2.0 or 3.0 | assume required unless the author says otherwise | yes | yes (GPL) | - | the credit line, and check the GPL reach |
| CC BY-NC | required | **no**: a sold game is commercial | no | - | do not use in a sold mod |
| Custom terms (e.g. Pipoya) | read the pack's own text | check | check | check | "not redistribute": a shipped mod is redistribution, so do not ship |

- **Multiple licences**: choose one listed licence and follow it; the most permissive one avoids share-alike [read].
- **Freesound** licences are **per sound**, and the page's right-hand panel shows them. CC0 needs no credit; CC BY needs
  the sound named, its author, a link and the licence in the credits, and the FAQ gives a sample line [read].
- **Retired licence**: Freesound's Sampling+ is retired; treat any old sound with it as CC BY-NC-like [read].

**Verified creator terms (this round):**
- 0x72 DungeonTileset II: CC0, "You can use this tileset for whatever you like", credit optional, 16×16 [read].
- Merchant Shade's 16x16 Puny World: CC0, "Feel free to use this for your game (commercially or not)", no credit needed [read].
- Puny dungeon tileset on OpenGameArt: CC0, "LICENSE (CC0) Feel free to use this for your game (commercially or not)" [read].
- Pixel Frog: the creator's itch page states no licence [read]. OpenGameArt's "Pixel Adventure 1" is **CC-BY 4.0**, credit Pixel Frog [read]. The itch packs' terms are **unverified**; check each pack's own page.
- Ansimuz: the creator's pages carry no licence section in the reads [read]. Creator comments in search say "commercial games. No credit required", but these are informal and conflict with the page read; **unverified**.
- Lospec palettes: the palette list and its usage guide state no palette licence [read]. Lospec's terms give Lospec a licence over what users submit and say nothing on palette ownership [read]. Practical rule: use a palette only when its creator's page states terms; otherwise credit the creator and record the terms as unclear. Colours alone are not usually copyrightable (a scraper project's note; search, unverified).

## 4. Credits: the record the agent keeps
Keep one file: `ASSETS.md` (a log) and the credit lines in `CREDITS.md` (what ships). The OGA FAQ's recommended form, which
is also a fair model for any library [read]:

```
"<asset name>" by <author name>, licensed <licence(s)>, <asset URL>
```

For each asset, the log holds: name, where it is used (file, map or part), source URL, author, licence and version,
date fetched, and what was changed. Changes must be stated for CC-BY, which asks for them [read].

## 5. What the agent does, step by step, for one asset request
1. **Search** the game's own assets first (`mod art sizes`, `mod find`), then CC0 libraries, then CC-BY/OGA-BY.
2. **Shortlist** three candidates with their scale (px per cell), view (3/4 or top-down), palette and licence.
3. **Check** the licence on the asset's own page and the table in section 3. Reject a non-commercial or unclear licence.
4. **Test the fit**: render the candidate next to three base-game images at the same zoom in the game or a render.
   Scale or view mismatch means reject or use only as a reference.
5. **Download** only the chosen files. Keep them in the project's `assets/` folder, outside `files/` until used.
6. **Record** the source, author, licence and changes in `ASSETS.md`, and the credit line in `CREDITS.md`.
7. **Report** the choice and the reason to the person, with the candidates rejected and why. The person makes the choice.

## 6. Working alone: scope
- **Budget the scenes.** Count what a part needs before starting. Cut the scene that does not carry a GOAL (`director.md`).
- **Reuse before making.** A recoloured or re-cut base sprite is cheaper and more on-style than a new one.
- **Ship less, polish more.** A short mod that holds its quality beats a long one that drops it (`quality.md`).
- **Playtest in small pieces** (`playtest.md`) so problems are found while they are cheap to fix.
- **Say what is not done.** A known-issues list on the upload page is honest and saves the solo modder from over-promising.
